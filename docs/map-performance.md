# Geographic map field rendering

The map now uses an aligned latitude/longitude sampling lattice, quantized to discrete zoom-dependent spacings and capped at 64 forecast locations per viewport. Neighboring views reuse individual samples in a bounded 256-location cache for eight minutes. Weather and air-quality caches are separate. This reduces viewport-dependent estimates and network requests; it does not turn a model forecast into a local observation.

Four-corner bilinear interpolation replaces the old all-point inverse-distance field. Spatial buffers now hold four indices and weights per pixel, independent of sample count (about 113 KiB on a 390px phone and 380 KiB at the desktop raster cap). A missing corner produces a transparent local gap instead of extrapolating from distant places. Timestamp matching prevents cached samples crossing midnight from being read at the wrong index.

Air Quality uses US AQI bands and the CAMS global domain through Open-Meteo's separate air-quality API. The global model's approximate 45km resolution is disclosed; the map does not imply street-level measurements. Sampling never becomes finer than 0.5 degrees for this layer.

`npm run benchmark:map` compares CPU kernels with the historical all-point renderer. Pixel identity is no longer an acceptance criterion because the interpolation model intentionally changed. Unit tests instead verify exact reconstruction of a linear field, local missing-data behavior, stable nearby samples, AQI band boundaries and request budgets. Canvas upload, WebGL and real-device battery costs remain outside this synthetic benchmark.

Startup installs forecast layers at style readiness and keeps the atlas visible until a frame paints geographic features. It does not wait for every tile or optional label/source. Isolated tile failures preserve the usable map. Retryable failures get at most two recovery attempts while the map remains open. A ready renderer, camera, layer and forecast-hour selection survive close/reopen for up to two foreground minutes; backgrounding releases the closed renderer. City detail fades over the returning map instead of revealing the dashboard. Cold-load and WebGL context failures still fall back cleanly, with visibility guards, throttling, cancellation and stable controls preserved.
